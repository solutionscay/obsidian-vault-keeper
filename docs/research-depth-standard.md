# Research Depth Standard

This is the research preference for knowledge-vault expansion. Expansion work should build durable understanding, not produce a fast summary.

## Principle

Research is **corpus mining**, not summary generation. A researcher should recover enough of an author's or institution's actual body of thought that a future reader can understand its concepts, vocabulary, examples, development, tensions, and limits.

A biography, search-result page, or single overview article is an orientation step. It is not the research product.

## Required workflow

For each selected thinker, author, institution, or school of thought:

1. **Audit the existing vault.** State the specific gap being filled and search for existing notes, names, concepts, and source URLs before adding anything.
2. **Find the primary corpus.** Locate the official website, archive, blog, newsletter, article index, talks, interviews, papers, book extracts, and downloadable resources.
3. **Read substantive material.** Fetch and read multiple items from the corpus. Prefer the author's own words and primary institutional materials.
4. **Download durable artifacts.** When legally and technically available, download PDFs, articles, transcripts, or other useful artifacts. Store them in the configured external research archive, not in excluded vault paths.
5. **Build a source manifest.** Record URL, title, author, publication date when available, source type, access result, and local artifact path when downloaded.
6. **Synthesize across sources.** Extract recurring concepts, terminology, arguments, examples, evolution over time, tensions, disagreements, limits, and relationships to other thinkers.
7. **Mark evidence boundaries.** Distinguish direct source claims, close paraphrase, vault synthesis, and unverified interpretation.
8. **Write for future retrieval.** The durable note should recover the body of thought, not merely the person's reputation or a list of frameworks.

## Minimum evidence gate

When the official corpus is accessible, target:

- **Five substantive sources per thinker.**
- **Three or more source types** where available, such as an official archive, article or blog, newsletter, paper or book extract, talk or interview, or downloadable PDF.
- **At least one direct quote or precise passage** for each major concept.
- A source manifest and local artifact paths for downloaded material.

The five-source target is a quality floor, not a reason to pad a weak corpus. If fewer substantive sources exist, state that limitation plainly and explain what was available.

## What does not count

Do not use these as substitutes for primary research:

- Search snippets
- Generic biographies
- SEO summaries
- Wikipedia or third-party aggregators
- Unsourced AI summaries
- Lists of books or frameworks without reading the underlying material
- A landing page presented as evidence for an entire body of work

## Handling access problems

If an official page or archive is blocked:

1. Retry with a direct URL, PDF, publisher record, institutional archive, sitemap, or another official representation.
2. Record the exact access status or extraction limitation.
3. Limit claims to what the reachable source supports.
4. Do not fill the gap with weak secondary summaries merely to meet a note quota.

## Closeout test

An expansion is not complete until the note or companion research file contains:

- The knowledge gap it fills
- The official corpus and source manifest
- The substantive sources actually read
- Durable concepts and precise passages
- Cross-source synthesis
- Examples and practical implications
- Tensions, limits, or unresolved questions
- Clear separation of source evidence and interpretation
- Links to related vault notes

If a run is stopped by a time gate before research begins, report that the run did not start. Do not substitute superficial research or call the goal complete.
